# Answer guide

Use after reader and retrieval outputs have closed. Each row describes the answer supported by the supplied passages. Numerals, spelled-out numbers, unambiguous date formats, equivalent units and equivalent descriptions of the same facts are acceptable.

| Question | Accepted answer | Common confusion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| q01 | Jody Clark designed the portrait of Queen Elizabeth II. David Pearce won the reverse design contest with a rose, leek, thistle and shamrock. The four plants can appear in any order. | David Lawrence and Stephen Raw helped adapt the drawing. Their contribution does not establish either as the competition winner. |
| q02 | The records leave the mass of the largest individual stone in Canada's share unresolved. | Multiplying the overall bulk mass by Canada's agreed percentage would estimate a whole allocation. A single stone's mass cannot be derived from it. |
| q03 | Texas added 3,999,944 residents. Its U.S. House allocation went from 36 seats after 2010 to 38 after 2020. | Utah led in percentage growth. Giving only the two-seat change omits the requested before-and-after counts. |
| q04 | Andrew Jones struck the billionth new pound. Production had reached one billion of the new coins. | The 800 million figure refers to old coins returned by that time. |
| q05 | Canada supplied the OSIRIS-REx Laser Altimeter, or OLA. It mapped Bennu's surface shape or topography to help choose the sampling location. The mission used Nightingale. | A camera could image the site. The requested Canadian instrument measured terrain with a laser. A description that uniquely identifies the Canadian laser altimeter also suffices. |
| q06 | The national resident total included people living in DC. Apportionment excluded DC residents. A state could count U.S. military personnel and federal civilian employees overseas and dependents living with them if they could be assigned to that home state. | Apportionment did not include every civilian abroad or every relative of an overseas worker. A home-state assignment and the co-residence condition matter. |
| q07 | The number of old round pounds already melted by July 10 is unresolved in the records. | The number returned and the plan to recycle metal from many returns give no completed melt count. |
| q08 | Canada was promised 4% of the total returned sample. NASA planned to keep at least 70% at Johnson for future research. | The lower bound matters for NASA's share. The two figures concern separate commitments. |
| q09 | Each state received one guaranteed seat. For the next seat, divide the state's apportionment population by the geometric mean of its current number of seats and that number plus one. The highest resulting value takes priority. Equivalently, for current seats s and population P, compare P / sqrt(s(s + 1)). | Naming equal proportions or giving average population per seat alone leaves out how priorities were calculated. An equivalent formula using the prospective seat count is valid. |
| q10 | The threshold was 60 g. The excess was 61.6 g, calculated from a final total of 121.6 g. | The 51.2 g from the final pour was only part of the material. The question asks for the excess as well as the threshold. |
| q11 | The Texas state senator who authored the congressional redistricting bill is unnamed in this collection. | The history identifies Senate Bill 6 and the dates of passage and signature. Signing a bill and authoring it are different roles. |
| q12 | The overlap ran from 28 March 2017 through the end of 15 October 2017. The July update counted 800 million round pounds returned. The Mint planned to melt returns and use their metal to make new pound coins. | A reference to the start of 16 October is acceptable if it makes clear that the status ended as 15 October closed. The Mint expected to use metal from many returns. The update gave no count already melted. |
